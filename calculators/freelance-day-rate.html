How to calculate a freelance day rate

Setting a day rate is one of the most consequential decisions a freelancer makes, yet most people either copy a number from a forum or guess. The problem with guessing is that it ignores three things every freelancer runs into: paid time off, unpaid admin work, and running costs. A salaried employee's paycheck already accounts for holidays and non-productive hours — a freelancer's day rate has to cover them explicitly, or they quietly disappear from your income.

This calculator works backwards from the number that actually matters: how much you want to earn in a year. It adds your annual business expenses to get the total revenue you need to generate, then divides that by the number of days you can realistically bill. That last part is the piece most rate calculators skip. Nobody bills 5 days a week, 52 weeks a year — between vacation, sick days, sales calls, invoicing, and slow weeks, most freelancers only bill 60-85% of their working days. That's the utilization rate field, and it's usually the single biggest lever on your final number.

Why your day rate looks higher than an equivalent salary

If a day rate sounds high compared to what a salaried employee earns per day, that's expected — it should be. A freelancer's rate has to fund the weeks nobody pays for, the health insurance an employer would normally subsidize, the software subscriptions, and the hours spent chasing the next contract.

Use this tool as a starting point, then sanity-check it against similar freelancers in your market. If a client asks for a project price instead of a day rate, use the Project Quote Calculator; if you just need a quick hourly number without the full utilization model, see the Hourly Rate Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What's a good utilization rate for a freelancer?

Most freelancers bill between 60% and 85% of their available working days. New freelancers tend to sit closer to 50-60%, while established freelancers with steady repeat work can reach 80-85%.

Should my day rate include taxes?

No — this calculator works in pre-tax terms. If you want an estimate of what you'd actually take home after tax, try the Freelance Tax Calculator on your projected annual revenue.

Why did my numbers change when I switched currency?

The currency selector converts your entered income and expenses using approximate EUR-based exchange rates, so the figures stay equivalent instead of just relabeling the same numbers with a different symbol. EUR is the base currency the rates are pivoted through.
